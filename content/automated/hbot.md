# Micro-Hyperbaric Oxygen Therapy (HBOT)

## What is HBOT?

Micro-Hyperbaric Oxygen Therapy (HBOT) delivers concentrated oxygen at mildly elevated atmospheric pressure—typically 1.1 to 1.5 ATA (atmospheres absolute). This combination allows your bloodstream to carry significantly more oxygen than under normal conditions, saturating tissues and supporting cellular processes that depend on oxygen availability.

Unlike clinical HBOT used in hospitals (2.0+ ATA for approved medical conditions), HBOT operates in the "mild" pressure range designed for wellness applications, recovery support, and general wellbeing optimisation. The lower pressure profile makes it suitable for home use while still creating a meaningful hyperoxic environment.

### The Science Behind It

Under normal conditions, your red blood cells are nearly saturated with oxygen—they cannot carry much more. But oxygen can also dissolve directly into blood plasma (the liquid portion of blood). Under increased pressure, this plasma-dissolved oxygen increases substantially.

At 1.3 ATA with 95% oxygen:
- Plasma oxygen increases by approximately 50-100%
- Oxygen reaches tissues with compromised blood flow
- Mitochondria receive additional substrate for ATP production
- The hyperoxic environment persists for 30-60 minutes post-session

This is why HBOT is sometimes described as "creating a hyperoxic reserve" — the extra oxygen dissolved in plasma becomes available to tissues that may have been operating in a suboptimal oxygen state.

## How HBOT Works — The Mechanism

### Step 1: Pressure Environment
The hyperbaric chamber creates a pressurised environment (1.1–1.5 ATA). This is equivalent to being approximately 1-5 metres underwater — enough pressure to significantly affect gas dissolution in body fluids, but well within safe limits for healthy individuals.

### Step 2: Oxygen Concentration
Inside the chamber, you breathe air with elevated oxygen concentration (typically 93-95% via a mask or hood). Combined with the increased pressure, this dramatically raises the partial pressure of oxygen in your lungs.

### Step 3: Plasma Saturation
Under pressure, oxygen dissolves into blood plasma following Henry's Law. Plasma can carry dissolved oxygen independent of haemoglobin saturation. This "extra" oxygen becomes available to tissues regardless of red blood cell delivery.

### Step 4: Tissue Delivery
The hyperoxic blood circulates throughout the body. Tissues with good blood flow receive supra-normal oxygen levels. Tissues with compromised circulation (where red blood cells may not reach efficiently) can still receive oxygen dissolved in plasma.

### Step 5: Cellular Utilisation
Mitochondria — your cellular power plants — use oxygen as the final electron acceptor in the electron transport chain. With more oxygen available, aerobic metabolism operates more efficiently, producing more ATP (cellular energy) per glucose molecule.

### Step 6: Post-Session Effect
The benefits extend beyond the session itself. Hyperoxia-induced changes (gene expression, anti-inflammatory signalling, tissue oxygen debt repayment) continue for hours after the session ends.

## Safety & Contraindications

### Absolute Contraindications (Do Not Use)
- Untreated pneumothorax (collapsed lung)
- Current ear infection or sinus blockage
- Recent ear or sinus surgery (within 6 weeks)
- Certain lung conditions (consult your physician)
- Pregnancy (consult your physician)

### Relative Contraindications (Consult Your Physician)
- History of spontaneous pneumothorax
- Severe COPD or asthma
- Seizure disorders
- Congestive heart failure
- Pacemaker or implanted medical devices
- Current chemotherapy (timing matters)

### Common Side Effects
- Ear pressure during pressurisation (relieved by yawning, swallowing)
- Mild fatigue post-session (transient, often reported as positive)
- Temporary nearsightedness (reversible after stopping sessions)

### Session Safety Tips
- Clear your ears early and often during pressurisation
- Never hold your breath during pressure changes
- Remove all electronic devices before entering
- Communicate with the operator if you feel discomfort

## HBOT vs. Clinical HBOT — Understanding the Difference

| Characteristic | HBOT (Wellness) | Clinical HBOT (Medical) |
|---------------|------------------|------------------------|
| Pressure | 1.1–1.5 ATA | 2.0–3.0 ATA |
| Setting | Home, wellness centres | Hospital, clinics |
| Oversight | Self-directed with guidance | Medical supervision |
| Indications | Wellness, recovery support | FDA-approved medical conditions |
| Cost per session | Significantly lower | Significantly higher |
| Session frequency | Regular ongoing use | Condition-specific protocols |

Clinical HBOT is used for approved medical indications (wound healing, carbon monoxide poisoning, decompression sickness) under physician supervision. HBOT is designed for wellness applications where the goal is general health optimisation, not treatment of specific medical conditions.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How does it feel inside the chamber?
Most people describe it as similar to being on an aeroplane during descent. You'll feel pressure in your ears, which is relieved by yawning or swallowing. Once at pressure, it feels normal — you can read, listen to music, or simply relax.
